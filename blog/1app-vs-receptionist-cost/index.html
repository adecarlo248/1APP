Small Business Automation Software vs Receptionist: The Real Monthly Cost

The wage is only the beginning. In Ontario, the general minimum wage is $17.60 per hour from October 2025 to September 2026, according to the Ontario government. A full-time employee at that rate is already about $36,600 per year before employer costs. A capable receptionist who can answer calls, manage schedules, deal with angry customers, handle quote requests, and keep the owner organized usually costs more than minimum wage.

For context, the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics lists receptionists at a 2024 median pay of $37,230 per year, or $17.90 per hour. That number is useful because it shows the baseline: even before benefits, payroll burden, turnover, and training, a receptionist is a major fixed cost.

The uncomfortable math: if a receptionist costs $4,000 per month and your average job is $1,000, that person needs to protect or create at least four extra booked jobs every month just to break even. Automation often needs to recover only one job to pay for itself.

What a Receptionist Does Well

A good receptionist is not useless. In the right business, a strong front desk person can be a huge advantage. They can calm down frustrated customers, handle unusual requests, collect details a script might miss, help with paperwork, and create a warmer human experience. If you run a clinic, showroom, busy office, or high-volume service counter, a person at the front desk can be necessary.

For trades businesses, though, the question is not “are people valuable?” The question is: “Do I need a full-time person, or do I need a reliable system that catches every opportunity?” A plumber, HVAC company, roofer, electrician, painter, or lawn care company usually does not need someone sitting at a desk all day. They need every caller acknowledged immediately, every estimate request followed up, every booking confirmed, and every completed job asked for a review.

Where a receptionist starts to break down

  • After-hours calls: emergency and quote calls often happen evenings and weekends.
  • Peak season spikes: one person cannot answer five calls at once after a storm or heat wave.
  • Follow-up discipline: manual follow-up is easy to forget when the phone is ringing.
  • Review requests: most teams ask once, then stop.
  • Data entry: call notes, booking updates, tags, and pipeline stages get messy fast.

None of those are character problems. They are workflow problems. That is exactly where automation wins.

When to Choose Automation First

Choose automation first if your business has lead leakage but not enough administrative volume to keep a full-time person productive. That describes a lot of trades businesses between one and fifteen trucks. The owner is busy in the field, the spouse or admin person is helping part-time, and the business is big enough to miss opportunities but not quite big enough to justify another salary.

When Hiring a Receptionist Makes More Sense

Hire a receptionist when the role is truly human and operational, not just reactive. If customers walk into your location all day, if you need someone coordinating crews minute by minute, or if your phones are busy enough to keep a trained person productive for a full shift, hiring can make sense. The mistake is hiring too early because the owner is tired of missing calls, when the better fix is a simpler automated intake and follow-up system.

The best sequence for most small contractors is: automate first, stabilize the lead flow, understand your numbers, then hire when the admin workload is large enough to justify the salary. That way, your first employee is not walking into chaos. They are stepping into a system.

Frequently Asked Questions About Small Business Automation Software vs Receptionists

Is small business automation software cheaper than hiring a receptionist?

For most small trades businesses, yes. A full-time receptionist usually costs thousands per month once wages, payroll costs, training, coverage, and turnover are included. Small business automation software like 1APP starts much lower and covers missed calls, text follow-up, booking, reminders, and reviews 24/7.

Can AI automation replace a receptionist?

AI automation can replace many repetitive receptionist tasks: answering common questions, texting missed callers, booking appointments, sending reminders, and requesting reviews. It does not replace complex human judgment, but it can remove the need for a full-time front desk role in many small service businesses.

What does a receptionist cost a small business in Ontario?

At Ontario's 2025 general minimum wage of $17.60 per hour, a full-time employee is already over $36,000 per year before employer costs. In practice, a capable receptionist often costs far more once wages above minimum, payroll burden, training, vacation coverage, sick days, and management time are included.

What tasks should a small business automate first?

Start with the tasks closest to revenue: missed call text back, instant lead response, automated appointment booking, booking reminders, lead follow-up, and Google review requests. These automations protect revenue before you automate back-office work.

When should a business hire a receptionist instead of automation?

Hire a receptionist when you have high in-person front desk volume, complex customer interactions that require judgment, or enough call volume to keep someone productive all day. Use automation first when your main problem is missed calls, slow follow-up, no-shows, and inconsistent booking.
